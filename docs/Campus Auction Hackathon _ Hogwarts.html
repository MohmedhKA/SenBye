The story

It’s the last day of the final semester exams. Seniors are packing up, leaving behind cycles, calculators, and books. Most of them leave campus within a week. Juniors need exactly those things, but today they’re sold through chat groups where the fastest person wins, and the price is a guess.

Build the place where campus competes fairly for them, and every item finds its next owner.

The basics

Every app must do these first.

Listings

Each item is its own auction.

  • A seller lists an item with title, category (for example cycle, calculator, books, electronics), description, condition, photos, starting price, minimum bid increment, and a closing date and time for that item. Different items close at different times.
  • A seller can edit or cancel a listing.

Browsing

  • Students search by keyword and filter by category, condition, price, and closing soon.
  • Each auction shows the item details, the current leading bid, the full bid history, and its own countdown.

Bidding

  • A bid must be at least the starting price (first bid) or the current price plus the minimum increment.
  • Students can’t bid after closing.
  • Every bid is recorded with bidder, amount, and time. All bids and maximums are public; nothing about bidding is private.

Closing and sale

  • When an item’s closing time passes, bidding stops. The highest valid bid becomes the provisional winner. If nobody bid, the item is marked unsold — that’s fine; the seller would just list it again by hand.
  • The seller approves or rejects the winning price. Approval reserves the item for the winner and confirmed campus handover completes the sale. Rejection, seller cancellation, and failed pickup are recorded with a clear outcome.

Notifications

  • Students can also allow browser notifications. The same alerts then appear as browser pop-ups when the app is open in a background tab.

Key features

Each one builds on the basics. There is more here than most people finish in the time, so choose what to build and in what order, and be ready to tell us why.

AI assistant tab

Some students don’t want to search and compare at all. They’d rather say “find me a cycle under ₹2,000” and get a straight answer, and maybe have the app bid for them. The assistant plays by the same rules as everyone else: it never acts without the student’s go-ahead, never goes past the limit they agreed, and never reveals other students’ account details.

Questions before bidding

Buyers often want to know more before they bid: does the cycle’s brake work, is the calculator’s display faded, are the book’s pages marked. Sellers don’t want to answer the same question ten times, and nobody wants an auction page full of spam. Give buyers and sellers a way to sort this out on each auction.

Never miss what you need

Juniors can’t check the app every hour, and the item they need might be listed while they’re in class or asleep. A student should be able to say what they’re looking for, such as “scientific calculator, up to ₹400”, and not lose it just because they weren’t online.

Last-minute extensions

If a valid bid arrives in the last 30 seconds of an auction, the closing time moves to 60 seconds after that bid. Everyone watching sees the new countdown.

Automatic bidding

A buyer sets a maximum for an auction, such as ₹1,800 for a cycle. The app bids for them only as much as needed to keep them in the lead.
